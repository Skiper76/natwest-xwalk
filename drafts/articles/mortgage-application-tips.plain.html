What to prepare before your mortgage application

James Okafor
3 August 2026

A little preparation goes a long way toward a smoother, faster mortgage application.

Lenders look at more than just your income. Here's what to have ready before you apply, straight from our mortgage advisers who review these applications every day.

Proof of income

Three to six months of payslips, or two years of accounts if you're self-employed. If you've recently changed jobs or gone self-employed, lenders may ask for a bit more context.

If you're self-employed

Bring your last two years of SA302 forms or tax year overviews alongside your accountant's certificate, if you have one. This is usually the fastest way to confirm income.

Self-employed applicants often assume it'll be harder to get approved. Having the right paperwork ready from day one makes the biggest difference.
NatWest mortgage adviser

Bank statements

Recent statements help us understand your spending patterns and regular commitments. We typically ask for the last three months across your main current account.

What we're looking for

Consistent income, manageable existing debt, and no unexplained large withdrawals in the run-up to your application.

Details of your deposit

Be ready to show where your deposit is coming from, whether it's savings, a gift, or an inheritance. This is a legal requirement, not just a formality.

Gifted deposits

If a family member is gifting some or all of your deposit, they'll usually need to sign a short letter confirming it's a gift and not a loan.

A realistic budget

Know your monthly limit before you start viewing properties, it keeps the search focused and avoids disappointment further down the line.

Don't forget the extras

Stamp duty, survey fees, solicitor costs and moving expenses can add up to several thousand pounds on top of your deposit. Budget for these early.

The applicants who move fastest are the ones who've already budgeted for the extra costs, not just the deposit.
