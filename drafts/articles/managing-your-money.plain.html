Five simple ways to manage your money better

Sarah Mitchell
3 August 2026

Practical, no-nonsense tips to help you build better financial habits, starting today.

Managing money well doesn't require a finance degree, just a few consistent habits repeated over time. We asked our financial wellbeing team for the changes that make the biggest difference, and narrowed it down to five you can start this week.

1. Track where it goes

Before you can change your spending, you need to see it clearly. Use the NatWest app to review your last three months of transactions and group them by category. Most people are surprised by at least one number.

Use category tracking

The app automatically sorts your spending into categories like groceries, transport and entertainment, so you can spot patterns without doing the maths yourself.

I didn't realise how much I was spending on takeaways until I saw it broken down by category. That one insight paid for itself in the first month.
NatWest customer

2. Automate your savings

Set up a standing order that moves money into a savings account the day you get paid, so saving happens before you have a chance to spend it. Even a small, consistent amount adds up faster than people expect.

Pick the right day

Time the transfer for the day after payday, not the day of. That gives your salary a moment to clear and avoids any awkward timing issues with your bank.

3. Build a small emergency fund

Even a few hundred pounds set aside can stop an unexpected bill from becoming a crisis. Start small and build it up gradually rather than waiting until you can save a large lump sum.

How much is enough?

A good starting target is one month of essential expenses. Once you hit that, you can decide whether to keep building toward three months or redirect savings elsewhere.

Having even £500 set aside meant a broken boiler was an inconvenience, not a disaster.
NatWest customer

4. Review your subscriptions

Recurring payments add up quietly. A quarterly check-in can uncover services you no longer use, from streaming platforms to app subscriptions you forgot you signed up for.

A simple audit method

Once a quarter, scroll through your last statement and highlight every recurring payment. Ask yourself if you've used each one in the last month. If not, cancel it.

5. Set one clear goal

Whether it's a deposit for a first home or a holiday fund, a specific target makes it easier to stay motivated. Vague goals like "save more" are much harder to stick to than a concrete number and date.

Make it visual

Name your savings pot after the goal and check in on your progress monthly. Seeing the number grow is one of the simplest ways to stay motivated.
